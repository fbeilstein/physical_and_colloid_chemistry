# Problem 1.

What is the conjugate base of $\ce{HClO4}$, $\ce{H2S}$, $\ce{PH4+}$, $\ce{HCO3-}$ ?

**SOLUTION**

**Analyze** We are asked to give the conjugate base for several acids and the conjugate acid for several bases.

**Plan** The conjugate base of a substance is simply the parent substance minus one proton, and the conjugate acid of a substance is the parent substance plus one proton.

**Solve**

If we remove a proton from $\ce{HClO4}$, we obtain $\ce{ClO4-}$, which is its conjugate base. The other conjugate bases are $\ce{HS-}$, $\ce{PH3}$, and $\ce{CO3^{2-}}$.

# Problem 2.

**SAMPLE EXERCISE 16.8**   **Calculating the pH of a Strong Acid**

What is the pH of a 0.040 $M$ solution of $\ce{HClO4}$?

**SOLUTION**

**Analyze and Plan** Because $\ce{HClO4}$ is a strong acid, it is completely ionized, giving $[\ce{H+}] = [\ce{ClO4-}] = 0.040 \ M$.

**Solve**

$$\text{pH} = -\log(0.040) = 1.40$$

**Check** Because $[\ce{H+}]$ lies between $1 \times 10^{-2}$ and $1 \times 10^{-1}$, the pH will be between 2.0 and 1.0. Our calculated pH falls within the estimated range. Furthermore, because the concentration has two significant figures, the pH has two decimal places.

# Problem 3

**SAMPLE EXERCISE 17.8**   **Calculations for a Weak Acid–Strong Base Titration**

Calculate the pH of the solution formed when 45.0 mL of 0.100 $M$ $\ce{NaOH}$ is added to 50.0 mL of 0.100 $M$ $\ce{CH3COOH}$ ($K_\text{a} = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$).

**SOLUTION**

**Analyze** We are asked to calculate the pH before the equivalence point of the titration of a weak acid with a strong base.

**Plan** We first must determine the number of moles of $\ce{CH3COOH}$ and $\ce{CH3COO-}$ present after the neutralization reaction (the stoichiometry calculation). We then calculate pH using $K_\text{a}$, $[\ce{CH3COOH}]$, and $[\ce{CH3COO-}]$ (the equilibrium calculation).

**Solve** *Stoichiometry Calculation:* The product of the volume and concentration of each solution gives the number of moles of each reactant present before the neutralization:

$$(0.0500 \text{ L soln}) \left( \frac{0.100 \text{ mol }\ce{CH3COOH}}{1 \text{ L soln}} \right) = 5.00 \times 10^{-3} \text{ mol }\ce{CH3COOH}$$

$$(0.0450 \text{ L soln}) \left( \frac{0.100 \text{ mol }\ce{NaOH}}{1 \text{ L soln}} \right) = 4.50 \times 10^{-3} \text{ mol }\ce{NaOH}$$

The $4.50 \times 10^{-3}$ of $\ce{NaOH}$ consumes $4.50 \times 10^{-3}$ of $\ce{CH3COOH}$:

|  | $\ce{CH3COOH(aq)}$ | $+ \quad \ce{OH-(aq)}$ | $\longrightarrow \quad \ce{CH3COO-(aq)}$ | $+ \quad \ce{H2O(l)}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before reaction (mol) | $5.00 \times 10^{-3}$ | $4.50 \times 10^{-3}$ | $0$ | — |
| Change (limiting reactant) (mol) | $-4.50 \times 10^{-3}$ | $-4.50 \times 10^{-3}$ | $+4.50 \times 10^{-3}$ |  |
| After reaction (mol) | $0.50 \times 10^{-3}$ | $0$ | $4.50 \times 10^{-3}$ | — |

The total volume of the solution is     $45.0 \text{ mL} + 50.0 \text{ mL} = 95.0 \text{ mL} = 0.0950 \text{ L}$

The resulting molarities of $\ce{CH3COOH}$ and $\ce{CH3COO-}$ after the reaction are therefore

$$[\ce{CH3COOH}] = \frac{0.50 \times 10^{-3} \text{ mol}}{0.0950 \text{ L}} = 0.0053 \ M$$

$$[\ce{CH3COO-}] = \frac{4.50 \times 10^{-3} \text{ mol}}{0.0950 \text{ L}} = 0.0474 \ M$$

*Equilibrium Calculation:* The equilibrium between $\ce{CH3COOH}$ and $\ce{CH3COO-}$ must obey the equilibrium-constant expression for $\ce{CH3COOH}$:

$$K_a = \frac{[\ce{H+}][\ce{CH3COO-}]}{[\ce{CH3COOH}]} = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$$

Solving for $[\ce{H+}]$ gives

$$[\ce{H+}] = K_a \times \frac{[\ce{CH3COOH}]}{[\ce{CH3COO-}]} = (1.8 \times 10^{-5}) \times \left(\frac{0.0053}{0.0474}\right) = 2.0 \times 10^{-6} \ M$$

$$\text{pH} = -\log(2.0 \times 10^{-6}) = 5.70$$

**Comment** We could have solved for pH equally well using the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation in the last step.
